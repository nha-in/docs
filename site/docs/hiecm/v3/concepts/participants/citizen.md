# Citizen

A **Citizen** is an individual who uses ABDM services to create, manage, and share their digital health information. Citizens remain in control of their health records and can decide when, how, and with whom their health information is shared through consent-based mechanisms.

## Identity in ABDM

Every citizen can be identified within the ABDM ecosystem through an **Ayushman Bharat Health Account ([ABHA](/docs/hiecm/v3/getting-started/glossary#abha))**.

### ABHA Number

A unique 14-digit identifier assigned to an individual after successful identity verification.

**Example:** `91-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX`

### ABHA Address

A unique, user-friendly identifier used for digital health data exchange.

**Example:** `name@abdm`

The ABHA Address enables healthcare facilities and ABDM-compliant applications to discover and securely exchange health information with the citizen, subject to valid consent.

## Creating an ABHA

Citizens can create an ABHA through approved authentication methods such as:

- Aadhaar-based [OTP](/docs/hiecm/v3/getting-started/glossary#otp) authentication
- Aadhaar biometric authentication
- Face authentication
- Other ABDM-supported identity verification methods

Upon successful verification, an ABHA Number and ABHA Address are created.

## Health Record Management

Citizens may use a Personal Health Record ([PHR](/docs/hiecm/v3/getting-started/glossary#phr)) application to access and manage their digital health information.

Through a PHR application, citizens can:

- View and manage their ABHA profile
- Receive and manage consent requests
- Link health records from participating healthcare providers
- Access and view linked health records

Health records continue to remain with the healthcare providers that generate them and are shared only upon citizen consent.

## Consent Management

ABDM follows a consent-based framework for health information exchange.

Citizens can:

- Review consent requests received from healthcare providers or health information users.
- Understand the purpose, duration, and scope of data access being requested.
- Approve or deny consent requests.
- Revoke previously granted consent at any time, subject to applicable policies and technical constraints.

All health information sharing within ABDM is governed by citizen consent.

## What Citizens Can Do

### Create and Manage ABHA

- Create an ABHA account.
- Manage ABHA profile details.
- Maintain and access ABHA credentials.

### Link Health Records

- Discover and link health records from participating healthcare facilities.
- Consolidate records from multiple healthcare providers through ABDM-enabled applications.

### Share Information Securely

- Share health information digitally through consent-based workflows.
- Use QR-code-based and other ABDM-supported mechanisms for sharing profile details where applicable.

### Manage Consents

- View active and historical consent requests.
- Grant or deny consent.
- Revoke consent when required.

### Access Health History

- View linked health records through ABDM-enabled applications.
- Maintain a longitudinal view of healthcare interactions across participating providers.

## Privacy and Security

ABDM is designed to enable secure and consent-based exchange of health information.

Key principles include:

- Health records remain with the healthcare provider that generates them.
- Citizens retain control over access to their health information.
- Health information is shared only after obtaining valid consent.
- Data exchange activities are auditable and traceable.
- Access permissions are purpose-specific and time-bound.

## Benefits for Citizens

- Unified access to health records across participating healthcare providers.
- Secure and paperless exchange of health information.
- Greater control over personal health data.
- Transparent and consent-driven data sharing.
- Improved continuity of care across healthcare systems.

This enables citizens to securely manage their digital health journey while maintaining control over their personal health information in accordance with ABDM principles.
